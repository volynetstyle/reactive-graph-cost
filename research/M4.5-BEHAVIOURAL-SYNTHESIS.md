# M4.5: behavioural synthesis and the boundary of the reference model

Sources: [M1](M1-QUANT-SYNTHESIS.md), [M2](M2-ALIEN-SYNTHESIS.md),
[M3](M3-POLYFILL-SYNTHESIS.md), [M4A](M4A-WPF-SYNTHESIS.md), [M4B](M4B-SALSA-SYNTHESIS.md),
[Alien flat control](M4.5-ALIEN-FLAT-CONTROL.md).

[CONTRACT.md](../CONTRACT.md) stays frozen as the original hypothesis. No L0 v2 is proposed.

**Result.** Behaviour identifies required capabilities, not their substrate. Across Quant,
Alien, the Signals polyfill, WPF and Salsa, every tested observable behaviour was reproduced
with client-owned state. L0 has not shown semantic necessity. That does not reject it as a
primitive, since almost any VM primitive can be emulated in user code. Whether any of these
capabilities deserve native support is a physical question, which this reference model
cannot answer.

## Why the behavioural route has ended

Two questions were mixed from the start: which relational capabilities real clients need,
and whether they deserve a VM-level implementation. M1 to M4B answer the first. A
behavioural differential cannot answer the second. A store and a client structure with the
same capabilities are indistinguishable through a public API, by definition.

For three clients this was true before any experiment: Quant, Alien and the polyfill hold
their own structures in their frozen sources. The experiments added what those sources do
not show: what a store representation costs, what is replaceable, and how far a flat
representation goes.

## Capabilities observed in clients

These recur. None of them thereby becomes a primitive.

| Capability | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| ordered one-sided trace or membership | all five clients, somewhere |
| ordered bidirectional incidence | Alien, by its validation and propagation walks. Quant by reading |
| push without retention | WPF, on the CLR |
| retention following activation | the proposal text, with design B |
| relation-local mutable word | Quant, Alien; both replaceable in meaning, and domain open |
| positioned insertion and reposition | Alien inserts; Quant moves; a variant of the polyfill and Salsa's B3 move |
| multiplicity of a pair | observable in Alien's residue, the polyfill and WPF |
| continuity of information after structural removal | Alien, WPF |
| persistent entry reuse | Quant as written. In Salsa only as an added variant, against a store rebuild |

## Representation-dependent

| Property | Why |
| --- | --- |
| epochs and retired readability | replaced by a client log in Alien and a journal in WPF, under explicit collection |
| dual incidence, for WPF and Salsa | one list suffices, and WPF's can leave the store entirely |
| stable relation identity, for Salsa and WPF | values and client records suffice |
| multiplicity in the store, for WPF | duplicates fit a client array |
| edge payload | none in Salsa or WPF; external in the polyfill |
| topology and retention in one object | polyfill design A separates them, and WPF needs no weak relation |
| linked structure, for behaviour | Alien passes on flat arrays |

## Two families of workload

| Family | Clients | Natural shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| trace-shaped | WPF, Salsa, the polyfill's dependency trace | owner to contiguous sequence |
| graph-shaped | Alien; Quant by reading; the polyfill's push side | bidirectional mutable incidence |

These are families of workload, not two primitives. Relation as an abstraction has survived
better than the Relation Store as the required place to implement it.

## What the reference model cannot decide

**Asymptotic bounds alone will not justify a primitive.** Alien's flat control is linear in
degree for `next` and known-handle removal, and linked records are constant. But the frozen
Alien source reaches those bounds with plain JavaScript objects. So the comparison that
matters is linked client records against a store, where the asymptotics are equal and only
constants differ. Flat against linked is a client's design choice.

| Open question | What would show it | Standard of comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| physical density | bytes per edge, per client family | the best client form: linked objects, or packed traces |
| mutation and allocation cost | relate, unlink, move, rebuild, at real degrees | contiguous rebuild and linked reuse |
| crossover | where rebuild beats mutation by trace overlap and order | both, at varied `R` and reorder |
| locality of traversal | walks over real graphs | linked objects and arrays |
| **GC integration** | a weak endpoint under a real collector, with no "before collect" hook | a client journal, which may be impossible there |

The last is the only place where a VM could know something a client cannot. Client journals
and continuity repairs were shown only under explicit collection: a collector running at any
allocation or concurrently removes the moment they rely on. Every "replaceable" verdict about
epochs carries that condition.

## Burden of proof for M5

M5 does not implement the accepted L0. It tests whether any candidate substrate outperforms,
or enables properties unavailable to, the strongest client-owned representations.

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| a native relation wins on density or cost for graph-shaped work | a relation substrate has a case, for that family |
| packed client traces win for trace-shaped work | no substrate for that family |
| only GC integration differentiates | the primitive is narrower: weak relational state, not a graph |
| clients win everywhere | do not standardize a graph substrate |

Any of these is a result.

## Limits

- Five clients, three of them signal runtimes. Quant shares ancestry with this research.
- Evidence is from small programs, corpora and logical counts. No workload, byte or time
  measurement exists anywhere in M0 to M4B.
- Two clients have an external executable oracle, WPF on the CLR and Salsa on its crate.
- Quant was not run on flat lists.
